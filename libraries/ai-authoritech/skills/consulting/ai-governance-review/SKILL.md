# AI Governance Review

Assess both control design and operating evidence.

1. Confirm scope, jurisdictions, industries, AI systems, lifecycle stages, risk
   tolerance, policies, and review audience.
2. Build an inventory of in-scope systems, owners, purposes, users, data, vendors,
   decisions, affected parties, and deployment status.
3. Classify inherent risk and consequence before evaluating controls.
4. Assess domains using
   [references/governance-control-model.md](references/governance-control-model.md).
5. For every control record design status, owner, operating evidence, test date,
   exceptions, residual risk, and remediation.
6. Distinguish absent, documented-only, partially operating, operating, and
   independently evidenced controls.
7. Identify deployment blockers, overdue exceptions, control dependencies, and
   decisions requiring accountable approval.
8. Prioritize remediation by consequence, likelihood, exposure, dependency, and
   time sensitivity.
9. Deliver with [assets/governance-review-template.md](assets/governance-review-template.md).

## Rules

- Do not equate a policy document with an operating control.
- Do not claim compliance, certification, or legal sufficiency.
- For jurisdiction-specific requirements, verify current primary authorities and
  record jurisdiction, effective date, source, and qualified-review requirement.
- Preserve evidence lineage and contradictory testimony.
- Require human oversight to be meaningful, trained, empowered, and measurable.
- Do not allow a high maturity average to hide a critical missing control.
- Treat missing ownership, inventory, incident response, or deployment authority
  as potentially systemic gaps.

## Handoff

Provide risk-tiered findings, control evidence, blockers, remediation owners,
target dates, residual-risk decisions, and inputs for policy drafting, implementation
planning, roadmap changes, vendor review, or qualified specialist assessment.
