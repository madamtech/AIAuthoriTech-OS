# Anhydrous Body Care Formulator

## Procedure

1. Define product format, skin feel, climate, packaging, use conditions, batch size,
   target audience, allergens, and fragrance direction.
2. Build the oil-phase architecture: liquid emollients, solid butters, structurants,
   powders or exfoliants, antioxidants, fragrance, and optional botanical infusions.
3. Verify that botanical materials are dry and suitable for the intended extraction;
   document identity, source, preparation, filtration, and oxidation risk.
4. Calculate the 100-percent formula and batch weights using
   [assets/anhydrous-formula-record.md](assets/anhydrous-formula-record.md).
5. Verify fragrance and essential-oil limits for the finished-product use category.
6. Define melt, cool, whip, rest, fill, and rework controls appropriate to the
   butter and wax crystal behavior.
7. Assess water exposure during consumer use. If foreseeable water contamination
   changes microbial risk, add packaging, use directions, testing, or preservation
   controls instead of assuming anhydrous means risk-free.
8. Define accelerated and ambient stability, freeze-thaw where relevant, oxidation,
   scent, texture, leakage, and packaging compatibility checks.
9. Apply [references/anhydrous-formulation-standard.md](references/anhydrous-formulation-standard.md).

## Output Contract

Return the 100-percent formula, batch weights, ingredient functions, process,
critical temperatures or hold points, packaging, test plan, fragrance evidence,
labeling handoff, risks, and formula status.

## Guardrails

- Do not call an infusion safe solely because the herb is natural or traditionally used.
- Do not treat antioxidants as preservatives or proof of shelf life.
- Do not infer fragrance limits from scent strength.
- Do not include fresh botanicals or uncontrolled water in an anhydrous product.
- Do not promise benefits that create drug claims.

## Recovery

If water activity, botanical dryness, fragrance limits, or ingredient identity is
uncertain, place the formula on hold. For graininess, separation, sweating, or
oxidation, change one attributable variable per bench trial and retain the baseline.
