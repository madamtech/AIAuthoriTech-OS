# Automation Cost Estimator

Use the [automation cost standard](references/automation-cost-standard.md) to build lifecycle scenarios without false precision. Record assumptions, categories, sensitivities, and totals in the [automation cost model template](assets/automation-cost-model-template.md).

## Procedure

1. Define scope, options, horizon, currency, labor rates, volumes, environments, confidence, and ownership.
2. Build a work breakdown for discovery, architecture, implementation, integrations, controls, tests, rollout, and adoption.
3. Estimate platform, API, compute, storage, network, model, observability, security, support, and vendor costs.
4. Include maintenance, changes, refresh, monitoring, incidents, retries, human review, compliance, and retirement.
5. Model volume, complexity, usage, failure, price, and staffing assumptions with low, expected, and high scenarios.
6. Separate one-time, recurring, variable, avoided, transferred, sunk, and contingency amounts.
7. Reconcile overlaps and dependencies; document exclusions, quotes, dates, and confidence.
8. Deliver totals by phase and category, scenarios, unit economics, sensitivities, risks, and update triggers.

## Guardrails

- Do not double-count labor savings and avoided vendor cost.
- Do not omit human exception handling or operational ownership.
- Do not use current vendor pricing without date, source, terms, and variability.
- Do not claim savings, ROI, or payback from cost estimates alone.

## Recovery

If material scope, pricing, workload, staffing, or dependency assumptions cannot be supported, do not issue a committed total. Preserve the scenario model, label the affected values as ranges or unknown, identify the decision sensitivity, and route required estimates or quotations to their owners before updating the baseline.

## Output Contract

Deliver a completed cost model containing scope, horizon, currency, work breakdown, one-time and recurring categories, variable drivers, operational and retirement costs, low, expected, and high scenarios, unit economics, confidence, sensitivities, exclusions, sources, owners, and update triggers.
