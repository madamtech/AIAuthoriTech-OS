# 3D Product Batch Ideation Architect

## Purpose
Make early-stage product creation fast, repeatable, and commercially useful by generating **batches of distinct but coordinated concepts** instead of one-off ideas.

## Supported Product Families
- statues
- figurines
- collectibles
- keychains
- NFC tags and bag tags
- badge reels
- modern wall art
- dimensional wall decor
- candle holders
- lamp bases
- sculptural tabletop decor
- display objects
- premium giftable decor
- coordinated accessories and add-ons

## Batch Modes
When the user asks for ideas without a count, default to **5 concepts**. If they ask for a bundle, collection, or product line, favor **5, 10, or 12 concepts** depending on scope.

Each concept must be meaningfully different in silhouette, construction, surface treatment, or functional feature. Do not create five near-duplicates with only color or text changes.

## Creative Intake
Resolve from the request when available:
- product category
- customer/audience
- theme or visual identity
- premium vs playful vs minimalist vs glam vs modern aesthetic
- size target
- personalization needs
- number of colors/materials
- printer choice: P1S or Adventurer 5M
- intended price tier
- assembly tolerance
- lighting, hardware, NFC, magnets, hooks, reels, inserts, or other components
- whether the concept should become a coordinated collection

## Batch Output Contract
For each concept provide:
1. **Concept name**
2. **Hero visual idea**
3. **Silhouette / geometry**
4. **Key design details**
5. **Suggested dimensions** or size range
6. **Color/material plan**
7. **Personalization opportunities**
8. **Print construction** — one piece, layered, assembled, modular, insert-based, etc.
9. **Production difficulty** — easy, moderate, advanced
10. **Premium-value feature** — what makes it feel elevated rather than generic
11. **Best printer route** — P1S/Bambu Studio or Adventurer 5M/FlashForge Studio, or either
12. **Next skill to use**

## Collection Logic
When building a coordinated line:
- establish one shared design language
- vary silhouettes and functions
- repeat 1–3 recognizable motifs without making every item identical
- keep a consistent material/color family
- create at least one hero item, one accessible entry item, and one add-on item
- look for opportunities to reuse bases, connectors, frames, nameplates, inserts, and hardware
- identify which concepts can share source art, vector assets, or modular components

## Premium Product Rule
A premium concept should show at least two of the following:
- sculptural form
- layered depth
- intentional negative space
- elevated surface texture
- metallic/silk/matte contrast
- hidden or integrated hardware
- refined base or pedestal
- modular/personalized insert
- lighting integration
- clean assembly strategy
- coordinated packaging/display potential

## Printability Gate
Do not treat brainstorm art as manufacturing-ready. Before promoting a concept to production, check:
- stable stance or mounting
- printable feature thickness
- overhang/support exposure
- assembly access
- hardware clearance
- color-separation feasibility
- expected orientation
- likely print time / waste
- whether the concept should be split into parts

## Routing
Use:
- **three-d-sculptural-collectibles-designer** for statues, figurines, collectibles, and sculptural figures
- **three-d-functional-accessories-designer** for keychains, badge reels, tags, and small functional accessories
- **three-d-premium-decor-lighting-designer** for wall art, candle holders, lamp bases, and sculptural decor
- **three-d-print-production-router** for final printer/slicer settings
- **lithophane-image-art-director** when the product incorporates lithophane panels or backlit image relief

## Recovery
If a batch feels repetitive, change the silhouette family, construction method, surface language, or use case before changing only decoration. If a concept is attractive but difficult to print, simplify or split it while preserving the premium visual idea.
