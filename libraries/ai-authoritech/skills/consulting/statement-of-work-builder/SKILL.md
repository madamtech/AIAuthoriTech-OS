# Statement of Work Builder

Draft operational clarity; require qualified review for legal effect.

1. Confirm parties, governing agreement, order of precedence, jurisdictional review
   needs, approved proposal, pricing, delivery model, and authorized approvers.
2. Reconcile every proposed commitment against the approved source using
   [references/sow-control-standard.md](references/sow-control-standard.md).
3. Define objectives and measurable outcomes without promising benefits outside the
   provider's control.
4. Define included work, excluded work, optional work, boundaries, environments,
   users, systems, data, locations, and volumes.
5. For each deliverable specify content, format, owner, due condition, review period,
   objective acceptance criteria, dependencies, and acceptance authority.
6. Define milestones, fees, expenses, payment triggers, and schedule assumptions
   only from approved commercial inputs.
7. Assign provider, client, and third-party responsibilities and required access.
8. Define project governance, status cadence, issue escalation, decision deadlines,
   and documented change-control triggers.
9. Identify security, privacy, AI governance, data handling, testing, deployment,
   training, support, transition, and completion requirements in scope.
10. Deliver with [assets/statement-of-work-template.md](assets/statement-of-work-template.md).

## Rules

- Do not invent or modify legal clauses, liability, indemnity, warranty, IP,
  confidentiality, termination, service-level, or dispute terms.
- Do not use subjective acceptance criteria such as “satisfactory” without an
  authorized definition.
- Do not make provider dates unconditional when client or third-party dependencies
  control the schedule.
- Do not hide out-of-scope work inside assumptions.
- Mark unresolved terms and conflicts; never silently choose the more favorable one.
- Require legal, procurement, finance, security, privacy, and authorized-signatory
  review as applicable before execution.

## Handoff

Provide the draft SOW, source reconciliation, unresolved-term register, review
matrix, and items requiring legal or commercial approval. Never represent a draft
as executed or accepted.
