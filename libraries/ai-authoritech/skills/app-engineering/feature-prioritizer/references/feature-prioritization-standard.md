# Feature Prioritization Standard

## Normalize before scoring

Require each candidate to identify:

- a stable ID, problem, beneficiary, and desired outcome;
- current baseline and evidence source;
- proposed behavior without premature implementation detail;
- success metric, leading indicator, and guardrail;
- strategic objective and decision owner;
- dependencies, constraints, operational impact, and effort range;
- confidence and the next review trigger.

Return bundles, vague solutions, or unsupported benefit claims for refinement.

## Select a method

Use a method suited to the decision:

| Method | Useful for | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Must/Should/Could/Won't | Scope conversations and fixed deadlines | Does not create a reliable order within groups |
| RICE | Reach, impact, confidence, and effort comparisons | Weak reach estimates can dominate |
| WSJF | Time-critical economic sequencing | Cost-of-delay estimates require discipline |
| Value versus effort | Fast early portfolio view | Too coarse for close decisions |
| Weighted criteria | Strategy-specific governance | Weights can encode politics as mathematics |
| Opportunity scoring | Importance versus satisfaction research | Requires representative user evidence |

Do not mix formulas and then compare their raw scores. Publish definitions and
keep the method stable within a decision round.

## Evidence and confidence

Classify evidence as observed behavior, representative research, measured
operations, contractual requirement, validated experiment, informed estimate,
stakeholder report, assumption, disputed, or unknown. Score confidence separately.
Use ranges for reach, value, effort, and timing when uncertainty is material.

## Mandatory gates

Keep these outside purely discretionary ranking:

- binding legal, regulatory, or contractual obligations;
- critical security, privacy, safety, or accessibility remediation;
- unsupported technology or expiring certificates and credentials;
- continuity, recovery, and severe reliability gaps;
- critical defects with unacceptable user or business harm.

Mandatory does not mean unplanned. Define scope, owner, deadline, dependencies,
evidence, and acceptance criteria.

## Dependencies and capacity

Model hard and soft dependencies, shared enablers, sequencing constraints,
specialist capacity, operational workload, and work in progress. Evaluate the
portfolio after scoring; a sorted list alone is not a roadmap.

## Sensitivity and tie-breaks

Recalculate with plausible ranges and alternate approved weights. Treat unstable
ranks as a research question. For close candidates, prefer:

1. mandatory controls and severe risk reduction;
2. higher evidence confidence;
3. an end-to-end customer outcome;
4. prerequisite or shared-enabler value;
5. faster learning or greater reversibility;
6. lower operational burden;
7. the named decision owner's documented judgment.

## Decision quality

Record the selected portfolio, items excluded because of capacity, expected
outcomes, assumptions, dissent, overrides, and review triggers. Compare delivered
outcomes with the original forecast to calibrate future prioritization.
