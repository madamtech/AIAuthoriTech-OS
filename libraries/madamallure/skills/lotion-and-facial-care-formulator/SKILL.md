# Lotion and Facial Care Formulator

## Procedure

1. Define use site, user, skin feel, packaging, claims, batch size, and shelf-life target.
2. Build water, oil, emulsifier, rheology, active, preservative, fragrance, and cool-down phases.
3. Produce a 100-percent weight formula with supplier-supported use ranges and pH compatibility.
4. Treat hydrosols, aloe, teas, and botanical liquids as water-phase materials with microbial risk.
5. Select preservation from the complete formula, pH, packaging, process, and intended use;
   do not infer efficacy from an ingredient list.
6. Define heat, hold, emulsify, cool, pH-adjust, fill, and in-process controls.
7. Require stability, packaging compatibility, microbial limits, and preservative-efficacy testing.
8. Deliver with [assets/emulsion-formula-record.md](assets/emulsion-formula-record.md)
   and apply [references/emulsion-safety-standard.md](references/emulsion-safety-standard.md).

## Output Contract

Return formula, batch weights, phases, process, pH and viscosity targets, preservation
rationale, testing, packaging, claims and labeling handoff, risks, and approval status.

## Guardrails

- Do not describe a water-containing formula as self-preserving without robust evidence.
- Do not substitute preservatives or emulsifiers by name alone.
- Do not recommend eye-area, infant, broken-skin, or high-risk uses without qualified review.
- Do not make acne, eczema, healing, anti-inflammatory, or other drug claims.

## Recovery

If pH, preservative compatibility, supplier limits, or microbial testing is missing,
place the formula on production hold. If an emulsion separates or drifts, preserve
the failed sample and change one controlled variable per bench trial.
